# Bone heat, as it ships (issue #284, Stage C)

Stage B found that only the art separates two strands from two panels of one cloth. The package already has a weight
rule that measures distance through the art: `rule: "heat"` (#161, `src/heat.ts`). This stage runs it unchanged on
every lattice mesh of `demo`, `scarf` and the trailer, with today's cut and with the continuous floor, in Stage A's
columns. It writes no new rule. Every figure is `tools/tear_survey.ts`'s.

## Reading

**Heat does not hold the line.** Three things fail it.

**1. Heat refuses ten of the 24 lattice parts.** The real `rig-parts build` with every lattice mesh at `heat` exits 1 on
demo and on the trailer, and passes only on scarf. Per part:

| example | refused parts |
| --- | --- |
| demo (4 of 9) | `hairpin`, `earring`, `neck`, `bottomwear` |
| trailer (6 of 9) | `hair_front`, `neck`, `handwear_r`, `handwear_l`, `bottomwear`, `hair_long` |
| scarf | none |

The refusals come in three kinds:
- **`RIG_HEAT_ISLAND_SOURCE` on dust.** An island of 1–2 px that the lattice meshes and no bone reaches: demo
  `bottomwear`, and the trailer's `hair_front`, both hands, `bottomwear` and `hair_long`.
- **`RIG_HEAT_ISLAND_SOURCE` on a real piece.** Demo `hairpin`'s third piece, 490 px.
- **`RIG_HEAT_BONE_SOURCE`.** A bound bone whose segment lies off the part's art: `chest` on both necks and on the
  trailer's `hair_long`, and demo's `earring` bone.

The trailer's `hair_long`, the part that began this card, is among the refused, so heat cannot be read there at all.
Under both heat rules a refused part keeps today's weights; each is named in the table below, as the brief asked. The
trailer's heat counts are therefore mostly today's: only `hair_back`, `hairpin` and `legwear` change.

**2. Where heat solves, it moves the idle far beyond 1.0 rig px.** No part's bone set changes, so the line applies to
every part:

| example | part | idle vs today (rig px) |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| demo | `hair_back` | 5.969 |
| demo | `hair_front` | 3.250 |
| demo | `topwear` | 1.632 |
| scarf | `topwear` | 3.854 |
| scarf | `hair_front` | 1.309 |
| trailer | `hairpin` | 5.477 |
| trailer | `legwear` | 1.705 |

**3. It folds the fringes.** Inverted triangles at 15°:

| example | heat | lifted reference |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| demo | 15, all on `hair_front` | 0 |
| scarf | 21: 14 on `hair_front`, 7 on `topwear` | 36 |
| trailer, 15° / film | 6 / 7 (all on parts heat refused, so today's) | 0 / 0 |

The fringe folds sit at the bang roots: demo x 391–447, y 98–168; scarf x 387–419, y 146–170. There a corner held by
the head (0.94) and one held by a bang (0.82–0.95) lie about 10 px apart. Heat is 1 on a bone's source pixels and
changes steeply beside them.

On scarf's `topwear` heat leaves 7 of the lattice's 35 folds. That is the one place it beats the lifted reference.

**The failure is the distance, not the cut.** With the continuous floor added, heat reads the same: 16 / 25 inverted,
the same idle figures within 0.15, the same refusals.

**Everything else holds.** Every bar passes, in this tool's check and in scarf's real heat build. Heat keeps at most
four influences. The bytes shrink: rig.json goes 471635 → 381298 on demo and 665178 → 525841 on scarf, because heat
gives a vertex fewer bones. The weights this tool writes for heat are scarf's real build's, entry for entry: no vertex
keeps different bones, and every weight and offset agrees to 7e-6.

**Time.** The solve is iterative: 270–1960 sweeps per bone on these parts. On scarf the real heat build took 77.9 s against
17.4 s for the lattice build, `topwear` alone 15.4 s. A first run on the same pool read 24.7 s for `topwear`, so the
figure depends on the machine's load. Demo `hair_back` takes 5.5 s. A refused part fails within milliseconds. Demo's
heat build is 22.8 s against 20.7 s, because it stops at the first refusal.

So the card does not become "which rule `propose` drafts". Heat as it ships fails on the domain (dust islands, real
pieces, bones whose segments lie off the art), on how far it moves today's rigs, and on the fringe roots. And on the
part that motivated this card it cannot run at all.

Generated by `tools/tear_survey.ts --page`; every figure below is the tool's. A tear is a pair of reference samples at most 3 px apart at rest (4-neighbours on the dense reference's grid) whose posed distance exceeds their rest distance by more than 3 rig px under some pose of the set, both skinned by the rule itself. "Inverted" counts a triangle once per pose it folds in (#265's convention). "Worst" is the largest distance between a sample carried by the written mesh and the same sample skinned by that rule, unrounded.
