# demo

A generated full-body character — prompted as an adult woman in a white and
pink frilled idol dress, with very long twin tails, a large ribbon hair bow, a
star hair ornament and dangling star earrings — chosen from eighteen measured
candidates to show what the
pipeline does with hair and cloth that hang. Its heavy inputs (the painting,
the two images See-through was fed, and the `full` and `head` See-through
layer sets) live in the
[spine-parts-examples](https://github.com/firejune/spine-parts-examples)
repository, which also holds the generation record, the candidate table and
the licence; `bun run fetch-examples` copies them into `inputs/` here
(gitignored). What is tracked is the light half: `config.json` (the
reference's config converted to this schema, `src/config.ts`; its `bones`,
`meshes`, `regions` and `motion` are the proposal's, unedited, while
`seethrough.head_box` and the `hair_back` plan entry carry the reference run's
two input-stage corrections), `proposal.json` (what the proposer wrote — the
`propose` stage), and under `expected/` the reference implementation's
outputs, for the TypeScript stages to be compared against — `parts.json`
(`assemble`; its `visible_px`, `occluded_px` and `visible_not_projected_px`, and
its top-level `recomposite` block of uncovered holes, are this port's counts,
which the reference does not write, inserted from this port's own default build
with every reference value left as it was), `rig.json` (the reference's, whose bones are all unturned; this port turns each chain link along its chain — issue #73 — so the built rig is compared through `flattenRig`, which turns every offset back, and matches it exactly), `motion.json` and `mesh_report.json` (`rig`), and
`check.json` and `gate_spine-html.txt` (`check`, the last being spine-rigc's
gate lines); `check.json` is
regenerated by this port's `build`, because the reference has no judgement lines
— issue #11 — and again when the turned chains moved a judgement figure or two in
the third place through the runtime's float32 posing — issue #73 — and so is `motion.json`, whose blink holds 0.084 s where the
reference's held 0.04 s, less than one 12 fps frame — issue #32; its four hold-end
and open-end key times are the only values that differ from the reference's beyond the rounding of its float64 sums). Issue #119 (`assemble` clears a fringe below alpha 250 where the painting shows the part beneath it, which the reference did not) moved these from the reference's: `parts.json` holds this port's values for the 16 parts the rule touched — 1,193 px pushed back, each part's `fringe_pushed_back` list by holder, `opaque_px` and the three visibility counts taken after it, the boxes of `brow_r` (374,… 28x9, was 371,… 31x12) and `lash_l` (35 wide, was 38), and `seam_override_px` one higher on `hair_back`, `topwear` and `face` — and this port's `recomposite` block (error px 10,340, was 11,050). `rig.json` holds this port's region offsets for `brow_r` and `lash_l`, the two parts whose boxes moved. `proposal.json` holds what the proposer writes on those parts: `brow_r` 2 px, and `hairpin`, `hairpin_tassel`, `sleeve_r`, `sleeve_l` and the `hairpin` segments 1 px, from before; `config.json` followed the proposal in those 14 values and nothing else, so its bones and meshes stay the proposal's, and `rig.json` holds this port's build of them too (8 bones by 1–2 px — `brow_r`, `hairpin`, the two `hairpin_tassel` and four sleeve controls — and the bind offsets and weights of the `hairpin` and `sleeves` meshes they drive), as does `mesh_report.json` (two `mean_influences`). `check.json` and `gate_spine-html.txt` are this port's build of them (23 reported figures moved in all, every status as before; the pack 94.9 % covered, was 95.0 %). Issue #126 turned every mesh triangle counter-clockwise in Spine world, the winding spine-rigc reads a mesh in (the reference, and this port before it, wrote them clockwise there). The declared exception, and nothing else: every mesh `triangles` triple `[a, b, c]` is `[a, c, b]` in `rig.json`; the `check.json` `TEXTURE_STRETCH` entries that print a triangle's vertices and edge print them in that order (20 here, 52 over the three examples; the triangle, frame and figures are unchanged); and the untracked `skeleton.model.json`'s `spine.sha256`, which follows the skeleton bytes. Every rendered frame of the build is pixel-equal before and after. `sheets/` holds the
reference's contact sheets of the two layer sets and of the assembled parts
(`sheet`); the reference implementation's rendered loop and README images live in the [spine-parts-examples](https://github.com/firejune/spine-parts-examples) repository under `demo/python-reference/`, for comparison only.
